Many Filipino young adults have heard some version of "Ikaw na bahala sa amin pag tanda namin," usually with a laugh. Children as a retirement plan, _yarn_? It is a joke because it is half true, and it is a question because nobody is sure which half.

This series starts from that joke. It follows a research proposal by four BS Psychology students at the University of Caloocan City, who ask whether two ideas move together among Filipino youth: _utang na loob_, a deep sense of gratitude for care received, and _obligasyon sa pamilya_, the sense of duty a person has as a family member.

## Two ideas, one household

_Utang na loob_ is often translated as "debt of gratitude," but the literal meaning is closer to "a debt of the inner self." The literature the proposal draws on treats it as more than money owed: it involves recognizing sacrifice and wanting to return kindness [@casino2025]. Studies of younger Filipinos have looked at how the idea lands for them today [@lim2022; @abogadie2025].

_Obligasyon sa pamilya_, family obligation, is the practical side: helping, supporting, caring, and showing up. It can mean money, and it can mean time, attention, or emotional support.

## Why ask whether they are connected

It seems obvious that someone who feels deep gratitude would also feel responsible. But "seems obvious" is exactly where research is useful. Gratitude can motivate help freely given, and it can also feel like pressure. Duty can grow from love, and it can grow from expectation. A correlational study cannot settle why, but it can check whether the two really rise and fall together, and how strongly.

The proposal stresses one more thing: utang na loob is meant to be given _kusang-loob_, willingly, and not as something imposed. That detail turns out to matter a great deal, and we return to it in post 7.
