Parents and children can use the same words and mean different things. "Tulungan mo kami" may be a request, a hope, or a reminder of everything they have done.

## Studies on both sides

Casilan and Baclagan explored parental expectations of children helping parents in lower middle-income families [@casilan2024]. Bernardo examined how utang na loob is valued within family relationships [@bernardo2021]. Ma and colleagues studied how family communication patterns relate to filial responsibilities among college students, with perceived social support and gratitude as mediators [@ma2026].

That last study is worth noting for this series: it places gratitude in the picture of how college students come to feel responsible for their parents. Its setting is different, so we treat it as context, not as a prediction for our sample.

## Why the study uses youth voices

Most of the research above looks at parents' views or at specific family types. This study asks young people directly, using their own answers on a questionnaire. It does not ask parents, and so it cannot say what parents expect.

## A reminder about scope

The proposal's sample is college students aged 18–25 at one university. What they report is how they see their own gratitude and obligation, and we should read results that way.
