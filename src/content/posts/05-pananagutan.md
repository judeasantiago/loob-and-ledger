Part 2 turns from gratitude to duty. In the study, _obligasyon sa pamilya_ means a child's responsibility to show care, give support, help, and fulfill duties within the family, financially and emotionally.

The word that captures the felt side is _pananagutan_: being answerable. It is the sense that when something goes wrong at home, part of it is yours to carry.

## The firstborn question

Two studies in the proposal's sources look at _panganay_, the firstborn. Baculi and colleagues examined firstborns' experiences of _pagtanaw_ of utang na loob toward family [@baculi2021]. Quindoza and colleagues looked at narratives of utang na loob among working panganays [@quindoza2025]. Both treat the eldest child as someone who may carry extra responsibility.

We should be careful here. These are studies of particular groups, and the proposal does not assume every firstborn feels the same weight.

## What parents expect

Casilan and Baclagan explored parental expectations about children helping parents in lower middle-income families [@casilan2024]. Chua and colleagues explored how Filipino family wellbeing is conceptualized [@chua2022]. Together they remind us that family obligation sits inside a family's circumstances, not outside them.

## Why measure it at all

Obligation can be a source of meaning. It can also be a source of strain. Before asking which, the study first asks something simpler: how much family obligation do young people report, and does it relate to how much gratitude they report? Those are measurable questions.
